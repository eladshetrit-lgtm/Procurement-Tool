The core trade-off

A supplier discount reduces your cost of goods — improving EBITDA. But holding more stock or paying sooner ties up working capital — reducing cash. This tool measures whether the profit gain is worth the cash cost.

The decision rule

Divide the one-time cash outlay by the annual EBITDA benefit to get the Cash / EBITDA multiple — how many years of profit improvement you're paying upfront in working capital. Below 3x: accept. 3–10x: escalate. Above 10x: reject.

Input reference

Offered purchase amount €

The total value of the purchase being offered by the supplier. Used as the base for all calculations — both the discount saving and the working capital impact of any change in inventory or payment days.

Current discount %

Your existing discount rate. Only the incremental improvement (new minus current) generates the EBITDA benefit — so this baseline is essential.

Current inventory days days

The number of days of stock you currently hold based on today's inventory levels. This is your baseline before any deal is agreed.

New inventory days days

The number of days of stock you will hold after doing the deal. If higher than current, the difference ties up additional working capital as a one-time cash outflow.

Payable days days

How long before you pay the supplier. Paying faster uses cash; paying slower frees it — the opposite direction to inventory days.

New discount offered %

The rate the supplier proposes. The delta vs. your current rate, applied to purchase amount, gives the gross annual EBITDA saving.

Calculations
EBITDA benefit  = (New discount − Current discount) × Purchase amount
Cash Δ inventory = −(New inventory days − Current inventory days) ÷ 365 × Purchase amount
Cash Δ payables  =  (New payable days − Current days) ÷ 365 × Purchase amount
Total cash impact = Cash Δ inventory + Cash Δ payables
Multiple          = Total cash impact ÷ EBITDA benefit
